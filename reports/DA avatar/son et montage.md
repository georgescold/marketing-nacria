## 0. Key points

1. **Keep the instrumental "original sound". Do not put trending songs under the voice.**
   - In our corpus, 19 of 24 viral health explainers with readable sound metadata use "original sound" (79 %). The other 5 use a cinematic or sped-up track. 🧪
   - The "+30–50 % reach with trending audio" figures circulate without a traceable source. ○
   - Business accounts are limited to the Commercial Music Library anyway. ◐
2. **Tempo has the best evidence of any music variable for our format.**
   - Expert debunking TikToks with **high-tempo (120–160 BPM) instrumental music** reduced misbeliefs the most (b = −0.56). High tempo beat low tempo (60–90 BPM) and no music.
   - Laypeople's testimonials did **not** benefit from high tempo. ✔ (Li, Li & Yang 2024, n = 873)
   - So: stay at **≈122 BPM** and keep the voice an *informed* friend, not a pure testimony.
3. **Moods: curious suspense resolving to hopeful.**
   - Creators' most-used moods are happy, hopeful and dreamy (≈30 M videos), and the top genres are electronic, alternative and pop. ◐
   - Epidemic's current trending mood is "Suspense", and its 2026 music trends include a return to organic real instruments and 90s-R&B-flavoured "PluggnB". ◐
   - In 6,152 Douyin videos, positive emotional valence raised engagement linearly. Arousal, loudness and speech rate followed an inverted U. ✔
4. **Level: our 9.7 dB music-under-voice sits right at the research floor.**
   - The listening-test recommendation is ≥10 LU of commentary over music. Non-experts preferred about 4 LU more than experts. ✔
   - Keep 9.7 dB (user-validated), but keep the mid-range of the music sparse.
   - Any music "lift" belongs in non-narrated moments. Music competes with narration for memory (g = −0.46) but not elsewhere. ◐
5. **Silence is a sound effect.** Words that follow a silent pause are better remembered. ✔ A 0.4 s music stop before the verdict is the cheapest "girl-edit" punch available.
6. **Sound effects work when they are synchronous with something that appears, and fail when they decorate.**
   - A sound synced to a visual change makes that change "pop out". ✔ (pip-and-pop)
   - Behavioural engagement peaks at *moderate* sensory intensity. ✔ (n = 14,492, Reels, TikTok and Shorts)
   - Irrelevant sounds and music hurt learning. ✔ (coherence principle)
   - Blinking highlights hurt. ✔ (signaling meta-analysis)
   - **Never use chewing or slurping:** 18.4 % of UK adults report that such sounds cause a significant problem in their lives. ✔
7. **The "girl edit" in this niche is a pen, not a sticker pack.**
   - What the women-led accounts I checked actually do (👁): a hand-drawn circle or arrow on the thing; boxed labels with a thin arrow; cut-out objects next to the subject; one keyword in a coloured pill; a serif headline; native white caption boxes; deadpan reaction faces; macro skin insets; emoji used as *icons* (✅ / 🌚), never as bursts.
   - Colour highlighting is the annotation type with evidence for understanding (retention g = 0.53). ✔
8. **Narration: "the big sister who did the reading".**
   - Conversational second-person wording improves learning (median d = 0.79, 14 of 17 tests). ✔
   - Young women prefer and believe health professionals (5.16 vs 3.95 credibility on a 7-point scale), but they come to TikTok mainly for "advice from others with the same condition". ✔
   - That makes the winning register a hybrid: warm, complicit wording plus explicit sources and an explicit fact-vs-opinion marking.
9. **What reads as teen or cringe to a 28-year-old:**
   - Gen-Z slang ("bestie", "les filles !!"), meme SFX (vine boom, rizz, record scratch);
   - word-by-word yellow captions, emoji bursts;
   - rounded or handwritten fonts, candy pink;
   - the "your cool friend" brand voice, which trade press now calls worn out. ◐

**Reading.**
- Usage data says *happy / hopeful / dreamy* for the mass of creators. Current trend data says *suspense*.
- Performance data in our format says **tempo ≥120 BPM helps an explainer that sounds expert**, and **positive valence helps engagement**.
- The best fit is a curious, suspenseful minor groove at about 122 BPM, built on organic instruments (felt piano, pizzicato, Rhodes, finger snaps). It should *resolve to a hopeful major colour at the answer*.
- Pure "aesthetic" lo-fi or slow piano (60–90 BPM) is the low-tempo condition that did not help debunking.

### 1.2 Original audio vs trending sounds, and the effect on reach

What the platforms say:
- **TikTok** lists "sounds" among the video-information signals used for recommendation, and weights them below user interactions. Its diversity rule: "your For You feed generally won't show two videos in a row made with the same sound". Users can hide videos "made with a certain sound". ✔ [TikTok newsroom, How TikTok recommends videos](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **Instagram:**
  - Reels ranking predicts how likely you are to "watch a reel all the way through", reshare, like and **visit the audio page**. "Information about the reel" includes "the audio track".
  - Low-resolution, watermarked and already-posted reels are deprioritised. ✔ [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained/)
  - In 2022 Instagram began ranking original content above reposts and aggregators. ◐ [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-updating-its-ranking-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-co/622424)

What is claimed but untraceable:
- "Trending audio = 2.5× views (Hootsuite)" and "30–50 % more reach (HubSpot)" circulate in marketing guides. I could not find either original report. ○ ([example guide](https://influenceflow.io/resources/mastering-trending-audio-on-reels-in-2026-your-ultimate-guide-to-going-viral/))
- Semrush's 2021 sample of 300 trending-feed TikToks found original sound underperformed and music-led videos did better. The sample is humour, dance and pets, and only the first 3 seconds were coded, so it does not transfer to voice-over explainers. ◐ [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/hooks-of-viral-tiktoks/)

Licensing:
- Business accounts only get the Commercial Music Library. Chart hits are largely absent. ◐ [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-changes-rules-on-music-usage-by-businesses/577734/)
- An enforcement tightening in July 2025 (unlicensed music muted or removed) is reported by a single source. ○

Sonic branding:
- In Ipsos's 2020 analysis of video ads, sonic brand cues were the distinctive asset most likely to be "high performing" (8.53×), yet only 8 % of assets used one. ◐ [summary](https://www.sixiemeson.com/news/ipsos-brand-audio-cues/)

**Conclusion for us.**
- Keep a generated instrumental, published as original audio. Name the sound on both platforms, e.g. « Ta peau dans l'assiette · thème ».
- Make the **stamp thud + CTA riser/impact** (already shared across episodes in `compte vidéo/sons/`) the series' sonic logo. Keep them identical in every episode.
- Do not try to ride trending songs under a voice-over. There is no evidence of a gain in this format, lyrics compete with speech (see 1.3), and the account is likely Business. ○
- One practitioner hack is to add a trending sound at near-zero volume. It has no data, carries licensing risk, and is not recommended.

### 1.3 Music volume relative to the voice

| Finding | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Listening test (22 listeners, TV documentaries). The recommended loudness difference is **≥10 LU for commentary over music** and ≥15 LU over ambience. Preferences were highly personal. **Non-experts preferred LDs about 4 LU higher than experts** (p < .05). | [Torcoli et al. 2019, *JAES* 67(12)](https://cris.fau.de/publications/234640600) | ✔ |
| A German broadcaster guideline cited in that literature: at least 7 LU of difference; listeners over 50 prefer ≥7 LU. Broadcast practice: music around −20 dB vs dialogue around −20 to −12 dB. | [Salford repository paper](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1488623) | ◐ / ○ |
| Moreno & Mayer 2000. Adding background music and/or sounds to a narrated animation is the classic "coherence" test. Later reviews summarise it as: learning improves when "interesting but irrelevant sounds and music are excluded". The median effect of removing irrelevant sounds is d = 1.11 (2 studies). | [Moreno & Mayer 2000, *J. Educ. Psych.* 92(1)](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Moreno_Mayer00.pdf); [U-Michigan summary](https://open.umich.edu/sites/default/files/downloads/um-multimedialearning-final.pdf) | ✔ (lab learning) |
| Meta-analysis (13 reports, 15 samples). Music raises attentional engagement (g = 1.06). The memory cost appears **when narration competes** (g = −0.46) and is near zero otherwise (g = −0.13). Advice: "a matter of placement, not avoidance": music up in titles, transitions and B-roll; reduced during narrated explanation of new material. Single author, who founded a music-for-video service (declared). | [Amato 2026, *Cultura & Saúde*](https://culturaesaude.med.br/en/background-music-increases-viewer-engagement-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis/) | ◐ / ○ |
| The model account measured on Demucs stems (12 reels): music 9.7 dB under the voice (range 7.3–11.5), −14.4 LUFS, constant, no ducking. The user requires this level. | `render_video.py`, memory | 🧪 |

**Reading.**
- 9.7 dB is within 0.3 dB of the ≥10 LU floor, and phone listeners are "non-experts", who prefer more separation. So:
  1. **keep 9.7 dB** (validated) as the bed, but make the *arrangement* leave room for the voice: no lead melody, no vocals, sparse 1–4 kHz while she speaks;
  2. put the music's *events* (motif statements, drops, lifts) in the voice's gaps and transitions, not under dense mechanism sentences;
  3. A/B test 11.5–12 dB on the densest explanation lines (§5f).
- Never use lyrics or vocal chops: background speech is the most disruptive kind of sound for verbal processing (the irrelevant-speech effect underlying the coherence principle). ✔

### 1.4 Music that builds vs constant

- The model account's tracks are constant in *level*. The "well placed" feel comes from the track's own intro → build, plus regular 0.33 s voice pauses. 🧪
- Arousal follows tempo and accentuation ✔ (Gomez & Danuser). Very high arousal costs engagement ✔ (Li 2025 inverted U; Xue 2026 below). **Build in density and accents, not in loudness.**
- **Silence before the key word:**
  - Listeners recognised words that followed silent pauses better in a surprise memory test.
  - The pause also changed how the word was processed (ERP N400).
  - The authors locate the effect in the "temporal delay" itself.

  ✔ [MacGregor, Corley & Donaldson 2010, *Neuropsychologia* 48(14)](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/listening-to-the-sound-of-silence-disfluent-silent-pauses-in-spee/)
- Practitioner consensus: drop the music for a beat before the punchline (the "stop"), re-enter on the reveal. ○ This is consistent with the above.
- Our validated ending is the CTA *climbing* to a peak (riser on « abonne-toi », impact on the "+", music +5 dB and held). Keep it. The new "stop" goes **before the verdict**, not before the CTA.

### 2.2 The "girl edit" SFX palette: what helps, what annoys

**Helps:** a sound attached to something that *appears* or *changes meaning* (pip-and-pop logic), soft and rounded.

| Sound | When it helps | When it annoys |
|---|---|---|
| Soft pop / bubble | A label, card or sticker appearing | More than one every 2 s; on subtitles |
| Pen scribble | While a circle, arrow or underline draws itself | Longer than the stroke |
| Marker squeak | A highlighter swipe | Twice in a row |
| Soft whoosh / air | A change of *world* (plate → inside the body → bathroom) | Every cut |
| Camera shutter | Once, entering the amateur selfie ("she documents herself") | As a transition |
| Paper stamp thud | The evidence verdict (our signature) | Anywhere else |
| Small chime / sparkle | One good-news beat (« les fruits sont innocents ») | On bad news; or more than once |
| Pour, knife on board, sizzle (food foley) | Under a food B-roll gap, low | Under speech |

**Annoys or reads teen:**
- chewing and crunching in the mouth, slurping;
- meme sounds (vine boom, "rizz", "bruh", record scratch, cash register);
- typing loops under the voice;
- risers before every line;
- the TikTok text-to-speech voice.

**Patterns across them.**
1. **The pen points at the thing:** a finger, a circle, an arrow or a boxed label tied to an object or a spot. It is never decoration floating in space.
2. **One emphasised word at a time,** in a pill or a highlight.
3. **Cut-outs and insets** (food, a macro of skin, a study screenshot) placed next to the subject. This is the "scrapbook" grammar.
4. **Reaction and humour beats:** a deadpan face or an ironic line, roughly once per video.
5. **Native-looking text** (TikTok-style caption boxes) beats designed motion graphics. This matches TikTok's "Reali-TEA". ◐
6. Emoji only as **icons with meaning** (✅, 🌚, 💃), and rarely.

### 3.2 Evidence on annotations, zooms and pacing

- **Signaling meta-analysis** (103 studies, 12,201 participants): highlighting relevant information improves retention (**g = 0.53**) and transfer (g = 0.33). It also lowers cognitive load (g = 0.25) and attracts longer gaze (g = 0.39).
  - On pictures, **colour highlighting** improved transfer, while pointers, labels and spotlights did not.
  - **Blinking impaired transfer.**

  ✔ [Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey 2018, summary (PDF)](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/CHU_KR-22_-Schneider_2017_Lernmaterial_Hervorhebungen.pdf)

  So we use: a highlighter in our accent colour, a circle that *draws itself* once, and no flashing or pulsing on explanatory graphics. The CTA "+" pulse is outside the explanation, so it is fine.
- **"Gen Z zoom":** a *jump cut* from a wide frame to a tighter one, as opposed to the gradual pinch-zoom TikTok calls the "millennial zoom". ◐ [In The Know / Yahoo 2023](https://www.yahoo.com/news/gen-z-zoom-how-to-203000205.html)
- **Doodle overlays:** white hand-drawn hearts, stars, arrows and handwritten notes over untouched photos, "spreading fast" on TikTok and Reels. ○ [Picsart](https://picsart.com/blog/ai-doodle-trend/)
- **Pacing (our corpus):** one new image every 3–5 s (≈12 shots/min, the optimum measured on 10,000 science TikToks), and nothing static. 🧪 Moderate stimulation wins (Xue 2026). Add punctuation through annotations, not through more cuts.

### 3.3 What reads cringe or teen to a 28-year-old

- **Emoji and slang.**
  - Gen Z respondents (UK, n = 2,000, aged 16–24 or 16–29 depending on the report) rank 👍, ❤️, 👌, ✅, 💩, 😭 and 👏 as emojis that make the sender look "old". 😂 is replaced by 💀. ◐ [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/life/thumbs-up-emoji-wrong-old-gen-z-survey)
  - Camille, at 24–32, sits *between* the two codes. Any emoji-forward styling is a generational marker either way. **Default: no emoji in the video; at most one semantic icon.**
- **The "your cool friend" brand voice** is described as so common it has lost its edge. Critics say "best-friend voice" often reads as condescending. ◐ [NPR / KERA 2025](https://www.keranews.org/2025-04-04/the-way-brands-speak-to-us-has-changed-a-lot-in-just-the-last-decade); [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.asia/?p=79943)
- **Our charte data:** rounded or handwritten fonts, rounded pills and candy pink are the teen code, only 9 % of her brand universe.
- **tendances.md:**
  - word-by-word yellow captions with emoji bursts;
  - all-caps sentences;
  - red arrows;
  - fake sensor curves.
- **Inference from the above:** Gen-Z slang ("bestie", "c'est giga", "le slay"), meme SFX, "POV" overuse, ransom-note letters and sparkle-everywhere CapCut templates read as teen, or as a brand imitating teens. ○

**Reading.**
- Young women come for **peers who share the condition** and believe **experts**. Experts gain from fast music; testimonials do not. Experts can feel *less credible* (cold, top-down).
- So the register is **"the big sister who went through all the studies"**: she speaks in « tu », is complicit, funny once per video and anti-guilt, and is firm and sourced on facts.
- **Margot has no diploma and is an AI,** so her credibility must come from:
  1. the named study;
  2. the evidence stamp;
  3. **explicitly labelling fact vs opinion**, which is the verification behaviour these viewers already use.
- She must never fake personal testimony ("moi aussi j'ai eu de l'acné"). She borrows experiential credibility through **the viewer's** experience (« si tu as déjà… ») and through comments.

## 5. The spec for our engine

### 5a. Music brief for Lyria 3.5 (or any timed-section generator)

**Why each choice:**
- ≈122 BPM: inside the 120–160 BPM range that helped expert debunks, without the hype of 140+ BPM.
- Minor key going to the relative major: valence comes from mode, and positive valence helps engagement.
- Organic instruments: Epidemic's 2026 trends.
- Snaps and Rhodes give the R&B, "girl talk" colour.
- No vocals: speech interference.
- Sparse mid-range: the ≥10 LU target.

**Prompt** (replace `FOOD_MUSIC` for this account; keep the timed sections from `episodes_nutrition.py`):

> Warm investigative pop-documentary instrumental for a women's skin-and-food explainer, 122 BPM, 4/4, F minor, lifting to A-flat major in the answer section. Felt piano playing a short four-note signature motif, plucked pizzicato strings, muted Rhodes electric-piano chords, finger snaps and soft claps on 2 and 4, light ticking hi-hats, a round warm sub-bass pulse, rare soft celesta sparkles. Curious, confident, intimate, a little mischievous, like a smart friend uncovering a secret; suspense without darkness. Clear and present, bright but warm, sparse mid-range leaving room for a spoken voice, no lead melody over the voice. Instrumental only.

**Negative:** vocals, singing, humming, choir, spoken words, lyrics, vocal chops, EDM drop, trap hi-hat rolls, horror, heavy distortion, music box, children's music, ukulele and whistling, lo-fi vinyl crackle, muffled low-pass, corporate upbeat.

**Arc over 60 s.** Lyria follows timed sections loosely, so the two stops are cut in the mix, not asked of the model.

### 5c. On-screen "girl edit" annotations to draw in code

All of them use the existing helpers (`_ease`, `rgb`, palette constants) and the timing hooks (`graphics`, `hud`, `tm.at(word)`). The common pen style is the existing `circle_doodle` stroke, used everywhere:
- line: 7 px lie-de-vin `#722A43`, with an 11 px porcelain `#F8F4F0` halo underneath;
- every stroke draws itself once, with ease-out, and never blinks;
- nothing ever goes in the right-hand 140 px column or the bottom 380 px.

1. **Pen circle** (`circle`, exists).
   - Keep the 1.12 turns and the wobble.
   - Start the draw 0.08 s *before* the keyword, so it lands on the word. Draw in 0.5 s, hold to the end of the line, fade out in 0.15 s.
   - One per shot, tracked (exists).
2. **Highlighter swipe** (`highlight`, new).
   - A rough-edged band behind one word of a card, a label or the hook: height 0.55 × cap height, on the lower 60 % of the word, ends rounded 6 px, edges jittered ±3 px, angle −1.5°.
   - Colour lie-de-vin at 25 % alpha on light backgrounds. On a photo, put a porcelain plate behind it first.
   - Sweeps left to right in 0.22 s (ease-out cubic) at the word's onset. The text is redrawn above it.
   - Max 2 per video. The evidence for colour highlighting is the strongest of all annotation types.
3. **Hand-drawn arrow** (`arrow`, new).
   - A quadratic Bézier from the edge of a `hud` label to its target. Curvature 20–30 % of the length; 2 px wobble (low-frequency noise).
   - Drawn along the path in 0.3 s, starting 0.1 s after the label lands. The head is two 26 px strokes at ±35°, drawn from 0.30 to 0.38 s.
   - Max 2 per video. This is the Glucose Goddess "label + thin arrow" grammar.
4. **Double underline** (`underline`, new).
   - Two slightly non-parallel strokes (5 px, 1–2° apart) under a stat card's number or a spoken figure on screen.
   - 0.18 s each, the second 0.08 s after the first, starting when the count-up ends.
5. **Myth scribble-out** (`strike`, new).
   - A 3–4-pass zig-zag across the accused word or a small food cut-out.
   - Lie-de-vin by default. Brique `#B0392C` only when the stamp is FAUX.
   - 0.35 s, with the scribble SFX. Max 1.
6. **Paper aside sticker** (`aside`, new).
   - A small porcelain card, 6 px corner radius (not a pill).
   - A washi-tape strip in sable `#E9E2D8` at 80 % across one top corner.
   - Rotation −3° to +2°.
   - Manrope ExtraBold caps, 30–34 px, lie-de-vin, +4 % tracking.
   - Text examples: « LE PIRE ? », « SPOILER », « ÇA, C'EST PROUVÉ », « 10 SEMAINES », « 32 PERSONNES ».
   - Enters with scale 0.90 → 1.04 → 1.00 in 0.2 s, plus a pop. It sits in the upper third near the left margin and leaves with the line. Max 3.

**Optional:**
- the **jump zoom** (×1.10, instant, max 2);
- one **cut-out inset** (a macro of skin or a food cut-out with a 6 px porcelain border and a soft shadow) beside the subject, the "scrapbook" grammar of Glucose Goddess and Samy.

**Excluded:** emoji in the video, handwritten fonts, sparkles on screen, flashing or pulsing on explanatory graphics.

### 5d. Narration notes for the TTS prompt (Gemini, French)

The memory notes that every added word changes Gemini's pace. Start from the validated `nutri_jeune` v3 style and add **only** the two marked clauses, then measure wpm and pitch spread.

> Voix d'une grande sœur qui a lu toutes les études : chaleureuse, complice, posée. **Un sourire dans la voix sur les apartés, nette et ferme sur les chiffres.** Environ 200 mots par minute, le même débit du début à la fin ; l'énergie passe par l'intonation, jamais par la vitesse. **Fins de phrase qui descendent, pas de voix cassée ni chuchotée.**

Script-side notes. Pauses come from the text: Gemini reads bracket tags aloud, and only `<laugh>` / `<sigh>` are allowed.
- **« Le pire ? »** stands as its own sentence, so the TTS breathes there, and the music stop lands on it.
- **One `<laugh>` at most per video,** on the deinfluencing aside, never on a figure.
- **Keep the existing pause caps** (« ? » 0.7 s, « . » 0.55 s, `voix.steady` against rushed relaunches). The verdict line is preceded by a full stop, which leaves the 0.45 s air.
- **Pitch:** keep Erinome at about 200 Hz.
- **No uptalk on statements; no vocal fry.**

### 5e. Five things to avoid

1. **A trending song, lyrics or vocal chops under the voice.**
   - Lyrics compete with speech.
   - There is no evidence of a reach gain for voice-over explainers.
   - Business-account licensing risk.
2. **SFX that decorate rather than point.**
   - A whoosh on every cut, sounds under subtitles, more than about 12 per minute.
   - Meme sounds (vine boom, rizz, record scratch, "bruh").
   - Flashing or pulsing annotations, since blinking hurts understanding.
3. **Mouth sounds.** Chewing, crunching in the mouth, slurping, loud breathing or ASMR whispering: 18.4 % report a significant problem with these sounds.
4. **Teen or "cool friend" codes.**
   - "bestie", "les filles !!", "OMG";
   - word-by-word yellow captions, emoji bursts;
   - handwritten or rounded fonts, candy pink, sparkle templates, ransom-note letters;
   - a hyped "tu vas ADORER" register.
5. **Losing the referee.**
   - Fake personal testimony from an AI persona.
   - Uptalk or vocal fry.
   - Music turning major and happy *before* the evidence is in.
   - Fake sensor curves.
   - Opinions not labelled as opinions.
   - Dropping the named source or the stamp.

### 5f. How to know it worked: A/B on our own accounts

Test one variable at a time, two episodes per arm, and compare each against the account median. Measure: 3-second hold, average watch time, completion, shares and saves, follows per 1,000 views.

1. **New music brief** (122 BPM, organic, minor → major) vs the current brief (dark documentary, 120 BPM).
2. **The two music stops** on vs off.
3. **SFX** on at the levels above vs off, apart from the stamp and the CTA.
4. **Music at 9.7 dB vs 11.5 dB** under the voice, both at −14.4 LUFS.
5. **"Big sister" asides** (fact-vs-opinion labels plus one humour line) vs the current script.
