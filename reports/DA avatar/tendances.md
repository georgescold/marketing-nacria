# Art direction for « Ta peau dans l'assiette »: trends, codes and colour data, built from Camille's tastes

Research date: 16/10/2026. Report in English, as requested.

**Goal.** Replace the current dark forest-green look, which the client finds "triste" (sad). The new look must come only from what the target, Camille, likes, and not from the app brand.

**Camille.** French, 24–32, adult acne on the chin and jaw. She is a perfectionist, documents her skin and buys at the pharmacy. She likes brunch, pilates, series, women's health and deinfluencing. Food rules and influencers selling things tire her out, and she hates "glass skin".

**Confidence tags**, used throughout:
- ✔ primary source or peer-reviewed;
- ◐ trade press or reputable media;
- ○ vendor blog or single unverified source;
- 👁 seen by me on public thumbnails or pages on 16/10/2026.

## 0. Key points

1. **Dark green is sad mostly because it is dark, not because it is green.** In Valdez & Mehrabian (1994), brightness was the strongest driver of pleasure (Pleasure = .69 × brightness + .22 × saturation). Green and blue-green hues rated among the most pleasant. Women react more negatively than men to dark colours (Hemphill 1996). ✔
   - The current covers measure **#0B110F to #1B201A** (CIE lightness L* 5–12), with an average frame colour of #24241E. That is near-black olive-green, the colour family people link with "dirty / death / tar". Pantone 448C, the colour on French plain cigarette packs, is in that family. ✔ (Pantone 448C, Palmer & Schloss)
2. **Data on women's feeds favours warm, light and saturated images, with red-purple-pink tones.**
   - On about 1 M Pinterest images (a mostly female audience), red, purple and pink images were repinned more, and green, blue, black and yellow images less (Bakhshi & Gilbert 2015). ✔
   - Filters that add warmth, exposure and contrast raised views by 21 % and comments by 45 %, with food photos gaining the most (Bakhshi et al. 2016 and 2019). ✔
3. **The 2026 trend vocabulary points to warm light neutrals plus confident colour, away from all-beige.** The 2026 colour references are:

   | Source | 2026 colour pick |
   |---|---|
   | Pantone, colour of the year | Cloud Dancer (an off-white) |
   | WGSN, A/W 26/27 key colours | Wax Paper (a creamy off-white) |
   | Pinterest Palette | Cool Blue, Jade, Plum Noir, Wasabi, Persimmon |
   | WGSN, colour of the year | Transformative Teal |

   - Pinterest and NBC report that "all-beige" is fading and that Millennials and Gen Z want more colourful palettes. ✔◐
4. **The content mood has turned to reality.** TikTok Next 2026 names the shift "Reali-TEA": "less interested in filtered fantasies". Its "Evidence Economy" means proof and community validation now drive trust, which matches our evidence stamp almost word for word. ✔◐
5. **Glass skin is under fire.** At the EADV 2026 congress, a study found about 95 % of viral glass-skin TikToks contained at least one misleading claim. The "clean girl" look is widely declared over. Showing untouched skin is both on-trend and true to Camille. ◐
6. **Every viral skin explainer I checked is warm and light, and none uses a dark green field.** The palettes are peach, pink, coral and plum: Lowkey Lit (41 M views), Mini BoBo (FR, 3.1 M), velvet glow (6.3 M) and Glucose Goddess's graphs. 👁
7. **For subtitles, use a humanist sans in sentence case with a solid backing.** TikTok Sans is now open-source on Google Fonts (OFL), described as "perfect for making your video captions stand out". Use editorial soft serifs for titles only. All-caps continuous text reads about 10–20 % slower (Tinker). ✔
8. **My pick is route A, « Carnet crème »:** cream paper, plum ink, a persimmon accent, jade-teal for proven verdicts, Fraunces titles and TikTok Sans subtitles. The two alternatives are route B, « Brunch solaire » (sunlit colour blocks), and route C, « Prune velours » (dark plum, closest to the current pipeline).

### 1.6 What to keep for the art direction

### 2.2 Patterns across the reference accounts

1. **Warm, mid-to-high-key colour.** Every skin explainer that went viral lives in peach, pink, coral and plum, which are the colours of skin and flesh. The darker tones are burgundy or plum, never green-black.
2. **One readable word or line at a time,** centred, very heavy weight, with an outline or a solid backing. Titles go in a top band.
3. **Real footage wins for selfies and food;** illustration and 3D win for the inside of the body. velvet glow does not even design a background.
4. **Wellness and nutrition authorities use editorial serif titles** (Glucose Goddess, Maria Marlowe) on white or cream with food photos. This is the "credible + feminine" code.
5. **Semantic colour zones are already learned:** pink for the spike or danger zone, mint for the baseline or safe zone (Glucose Goddess). Our verdict colours can reuse that grammar.
6. **What dermatology research measures on TikTok:**
   - physician content draws about 2.3× the mean views of non-physician content (712 k vs 312 k);
   - personal-experience videos score highest for information quality (DISCERN), though all scores are low.

   Credibility plus personal experience is the combination to aim for. ✔ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576439/

## 3. Typography for short vertical video

### 3.1 What is used

- **TikTok's native text** ("Classic" and others) is set in **TikTok Sans**. It was open-sourced in 2025 under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 and is **on Google Fonts as "TikTok Sans"** (I confirmed the CSS API). ✔
  - It is a variable font with Weight, Width, Slant and Optical Size axes.
  - It covers 460+ languages, including French accents.
  - Its stated purpose is "perfect for making your video captions stand out", "optimized for high-DPI mobile UI".
  - Using it makes burned-in subtitles feel native rather than like an ad, which suits Reali-TEA.
- **Creator tools (CapCut):** the most-recommended caption fonts are Montserrat Bold, Poppins Bold, Anton and League Spartan. White text with a black stroke at about 20–40 % thickness is the most common setting. ○ (vendor blogs)
- **Wellness and nutrition authorities** put large serif titles over food (2.1). In 2026 design commentary:
  - soft serifs (Recoleta-like) are spreading in wellness and lifestyle branding;
  - Fraunces, DM Serif Display and Cormorant have replaced Playfair Display as the go-to display serif, and Playfair now reads as dated;
  - beauty and fashion use wide editorial or Didone serifs. ○

### 3.2 Legibility evidence

- **Size.** Legge & Bigelow (2011) found fluent reading over an x-height of **0.2° to 2°** of visual angle. Below about 0.2°, reading slows. ✔ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428264
- **Case.** All-caps continuous text slows reading by **9.5–19 %** (Tinker 1955; 13.4 % in Tinker & Paterson 1928). Upper case can help for **single glanceable words**. Rule: caps for short labels and stamps, sentence case for subtitles. ✔ https://www.uv.es/mperea/lowercaseUPPERCASE_reading.pdf
- **Letterform.** MIT AgeLab and Monotype found that a humanist sans (Frutiger) cut glance time by **10.6 %** against a square grotesque (Eurostile) for men. Women showed little difference. Open apertures help when text is read in a glance. ✔ https://agelab.mit.edu/transportation-and-livable-communities/projects/glance-based-legibility ; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/the-right-dashboard-font-could-make-driving-safer-52838965/
- **Dyslexia.** Rello & Baeza-Yates (2013), 48 readers with dyslexia: sans-serif, roman (upright) and monospaced fonts improved reading, and **italics hurt**. Avoid italic subtitles. ✔ https://editingresearch.byu.edu/2020/05/28/which-fonts-are-best-for-dyslexia/
- **Subtitle conventions:**
  - Netflix: at most **2 lines**; each subtitle stays on screen at least **5/6 s** and at most **7 s**; never split an article from its noun, or a name across lines. ✔ https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements
  - BBC: prefers **one line** over two short lines. ✔ https://uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/10324/48376/3/revistas_uva_es__hermeneus_article_view_1737_3726.pdf
- **Contrast (WCAG 2.2):**
  - SC 1.4.3: 4.5:1 for normal text, **3:1 for large text** (essentially all of our video text);
  - SC 1.4.6: 7:1 (AAA);
  - SC 1.4.1: never use **colour alone** to carry meaning, which applies directly to the verdict stamp. ✔ https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
- **Why captions matter:**
  - 69 % of people watch with sound off in public, and 80 % say they are more likely to finish a video when captions are available (Verizon Media and Publicis 2019 survey ◐);
  - measured gains: Facebook reports +12 % view time; YouTube (Discovery Digital Networks) +7.32 % views, +13.48 % in the first 14 days ◐.

  https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/ ; https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/studies-find-captions-improve-engagement
- **Animated captions.** Claims that word-by-word "pop" captions beat static ones are vendor-only. ○ Untested.

### 3.3 Sizing and placement for a 1080×1920 frame

**Sizes.** This is my own derivation from Legge & Bigelow:
- A 6.1–6.7″ phone shows 1080 px across about 71–78 mm, roughly **14–15 px per mm**.
- At 30–35 cm, 0.2° equals about 1.1 mm, so an x-height of about 17 px, or a font size of about 35 px. That is the absolute floor.
- Moving text over imagery and glance reading need 3–4× the floor. That gives:
  - **subtitles 64–76 px** (x-height about 32–38 px);
  - **labels 40–48 px** in caps;
  - **titles 110–160 px**;
  - **stamps 90–130 px** in caps.

**Line length.** Usable width is about 860 px (60 px left margin, 140–160 px right margin). At 70 px in TikTok Sans, that is about **24–28 characters per line**. Use 1 line of 3–6 words, and 2 lines at most.

**Safe zones** (third-party estimates, not official):
- keep important content out of the top **~130 px**, the bottom **~320–370 px** (organic posts; more for ads) and the right **~120–140 px** (action buttons);
- place subtitles centred at **y ≈ 1150–1400**;
- keep the cover title and stamp inside the central **1080×1440** area, because the Instagram and TikTok profile grids crop to about 3:4. ◐○

Sources:
- https://www.remotion.dev/elements/overlays/social-safe-zones/
- https://www.aicarousels.com/free-tools/tiktok-safe-zone-checker
- https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/
- https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagrams-experimental-profile-grid-has-rectangular-images-instead-of-squares-160007086.html

**Compression.** Hairline serifs (Didones such as Bodoni Moda, light Cormorant) break up under H.264 and HEVC compression at small sizes. If you use one, keep it at 140 px or more and in a bold weight. (Practical rule from experience; untested.)

## 4. Colour psychology, with data

### 4.1 Lightness drives mood, and that is why dark green feels sad

- **Valdez & Mehrabian (1994)**, Munsell chips, Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance model:
  - **Pleasure = .69 × brightness + .22 × saturation.**
  - Arousal = −.31 × brightness + .60 × saturation.
  - Dominance = −.76 × brightness + .32 × saturation.
  - Blue, blue-green, **green**, red-purple, **purple** and purple-blue were the most pleasant hues; yellow and green-yellow the least.

  The *hue* green is not the problem. Its **near-black lightness** is. ✔ https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/
- **Hemphill (1996):** lighter hues drew mostly favourable reactions, darker hues mostly unfavourable ones. **Women responded more positively to bright colours and more negatively to dark colours than men.** ✔ https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8756892/
- **Wilms & Oberfeld (2018):** saturated and bright colours were linked to higher ratings, with saturation weighing more than hue. ✔ https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/oberfeld/downloads/Wilms-Oberfeld2018_Article_ColorAndEmotionEffectsOfHueSat.pdf
- **Palmer & Schloss (2010, PNAS):** colour preference follows how we feel about the objects linked to each colour. Dark yellow (olive) and dark orange (brown) are **among the least preferred**, through association with rot and biological waste. ✔ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2889342
- **Pantone 448C** ("drab dark brown", first called "olive green"):
  - picked in 2012 by GfK Bluemoon for the Australian government as the colour about 1,000 smokers found most repellent, linked with "dirty", "death" and "tar";
  - used on plain packs in Australia, the UK, Ireland and **France**, so French viewers see it every day on cigarette packs. Dark desaturated green-olive sits in this neighbourhood. ✔◐ https://hyperallergic.com/304550/in-defense-of-the-worlds-ugliest-color-opaque-couche/ ; https://businessofhome.com/articles/pantone-s-ugliest-color-may-be-used-to-fight-smoking
- **Universality:** colour-emotion associations are highly similar across 30 nations (r = .88; 4,598 participants; Jonauskaite et al. 2020), so these effects are not only Anglo-Saxon. ✔ https://isidore.science/document/10.1177/0956797620948810

**Why it also reads "clinical".** This is my inference. Dark green plus white caps plus red stamps resembles hospital-scrub green, pharmacy-cross green and courtroom noir. It signals authority and risk, not care. With green as the base, the "healthy" signal of green (4.2) is also spent on the background, so a PROUVÉ stamp can no longer stand out.

### 4.2 Trust, health and science

- **Blue** is the most-liked colour worldwide: first in all 10 countries in a YouGov 2015 survey, chosen by 23–33 % of respondents. Hurlbert & Ling (2007) also found blue universally preferred. Labrecque & Milne (2012) show that hue maps onto brand personality ("exciting red, competent blue"), and that saturation and value amplify those traits. ✔
  - https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/12331-blue-worlds-favourite-colour
  - https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/08/070820120720.htm
  - https://colab.ws/articles/10.1007%2Fs11747-010-0245-y
- **Green means "healthy".** A candy bar with a green calorie label was rated healthier than the same bar with a red or white label (Schuldt 2013, n = 93 + 39). Use green **semantically**, for PROUVÉ, not as the background. ✔ https://news.cornell.edu/node/279365
- **Femtech precedent: Clue**, the period-tracking app. Its slogan was "Confident. Scientific. Not pink." Red is its main colour, with teal, green and purple; its research found few women cared about pink. ◐ https://www.androidpolice.com/2014/10/10/ladies-clue-pink-less-flower-less-butterfly-less-period-tracker-may-ashamed-using/ ; https://fortune.com/2018/11/06/period-tracker-clue-ida-tin-2016-election

### 4.3 Warmth and femininity, without the cliché

- **Hurlbert & Ling (2007):** women's preferences shift along the red-green axis towards red, making **pinks and lilacs** their most preferred colours. ✔
- **But only about 10–13 % of women name pink as their favourite colour** (YouGov). Use rose and plum tones, not "girly pink" branding. ✔
- **Pinterest, mostly female users** (Bakhshi & Gilbert 2015, about 1 M images, controlling for network and activity):
  - **red, purple and pink images were repinned more**; green, blue, black and yellow images less;
  - highly saturated images did better, and black-and-white images worse. ✔ https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0117148.html ; https://hyperallergic.com/study-finds-color-impacts-online-image-sharing

### 4.4 Appetite and food imagery

- **Warm reds and oranges are widely held to whet appetite, and blue to suppress it** (Spence 2015 review, *Flavour*). The evidence is **mixed**. In an experiment with 448 women, artificially recoloured food (red or blue) was *less* wanted than food in its **natural colour** (Schlintl & Schienle 2020). Rule: keep food in its true colours and never grade the whole frame green or teal. ✔ https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.589826/full
- **Background lightness.** The same strawberry mousse was rated sweeter, more intense and better liked on a **white plate than on a black one** (Piqueras-Fiszman et al. 2012). ✔ https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2ed45797-6ae6-4c4f-addd-1972e8887337
- **Filters** (Bakhshi et al. 2016, 7.6 M Flickr photos, mostly posted from Instagram):
  - filtered photos got **+21 % views and +45 % comments**;
  - **warmth, exposure and contrast** filters helped most;
  - a 2019 follow-up found that **food and people photos** benefit most from filters, while night shots did better unfiltered. ✔ https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14622 ; https://bigthink.com/technology-innovation/which-photo-filters-increase-user-engagement/
- **Composition and colour variety:** for food photos, rule-of-thirds framing and **moderate** colour variety raised likeability; colour intensity alone did not (Peng & Jemmott 2018). ✔ https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/what-makes-food-photos-instagram-popular

### 4.5 Thumbnails and covers: performance data

- **Image quality** (professional-looking) raised engagement on both Twitter and Instagram. The effect of **colourfulness depends on the product category** (Li & Xie 2020, *JMR*). ✔ https://www.ama.org/marketing-news/from-pixels-to-market-outcomes-a-framework-for-image-analytics-in-marketing/
- **Koh & Cui (2022),** 3,745 brand videos on YouTube, treat thumbnail colourfulness and brightness as cues that work on the viewer indirectly, through first impressions. I could not read the results. ✔ (method only) https://scholar.korea.ac.kr/handle/2021.sw.korea/143319
- **I found no peer-reviewed study of TikTok or Reels cover colour and click-through rate.** The vendor figures (+35–40 % CTR) are unsourced. ○ On TikTok, the **first frame** works as the cover in the For You feed. The cover matters mostly for profile-grid browsing and search results. That is an inference; A/B test it on our own posts.

### 4.6 Contrast against the TikTok interface

- **TikTok draws its interface in white over the video:** the username, caption, like, comment and share icons and counts. Its brand colours are red #FE2C55 and cyan #25F4EE (the cyan value is unofficial). ◐ https://www.brandcolorcode.com/tiktok
- **White on a cream background is only 1.13:1**, so a light background makes TikTok's own interface illegible. Route A needs a **plum scrim over the bottom 380 px** (#3B1733, rising from 0 to 45 % opacity) and nothing important in the right-hand 140 px column. White text on plum reaches 15.5:1.
- **Do not let the FAUX red sit next to the heart icon:** #FE2C55 is close to our tomato and cerise reds, so keep the stamps mid-frame.
- **A light frame should stand out in a feed of mostly dark, moody faceless videos.** This is my inference, based on luminance contrast drawing attention, and it is a reason to break from the "documentary noir" pack. Test it.

### Shared elements, all routes

**The verdict system.** Each verdict is a **word + icon + position on a 4-step gauge**, so colour is never the only signal (WCAG 1.4.1). This matters because, in deuteranopia simulation (Machado matrices), jade and red come out close in lightness.

| Verdict | Icon | Light-or-dark slot |
|---|---|---|
| PROUVÉ | ✓ | dark, white text |
| PROBABLE | ≈ | light, dark text |
| PAS PROUVÉ | ? | light, dark text |
| FAUX | ✗ | dark, white text |

**The stamp:**
- Archivo Condensed ExtraBold in caps;
- double rule border;
- rotated −4° to −7°;
- rubber texture, about 8 % opacity noise;
- an ink-press animation of about 6 frames;
- the only rotated, textured element in the system.

**Verdict colours.** I moved PROUVÉ to a **jade-teal, #0F7C74**, because under deuteranopia it turns blue-grey (#676A75) while red turns olive (#7B7035). That separation is better than with a pure green.

**Timeline band** (top, y ≈ 150–250):
- a thin ruler with day ticks;
- a filled progress line up to today;
- a label pill such as « Jour 1 » or « Semaine 10 » in DM Mono caps.

It reads like a logbook (#lockedin, documenting).

**Selfies:**
- real, amateur, phone-native;
- visible texture, pores and redness;
- **no smoothing, no glass skin**;
- a typical chin or jaw spot of 1–3 lesions;
- labelled with a time stamp: « J+12 · 08:14 ».

   To a deinfluencing audience, they read as "someone is selling me something".
